The subscription cost strategy

Your platform will rely on dozens of external services — booking engines, payment processors, maps, SMS, email, CDN. Each has a cost model. Knowing when to switch, bundle, negotiate, or build in-house compounds over time. This is how Klook and platforms like it optimise margins as they scale.

Stripe → own payments at scale SendGrid → negotiate volume pricing Mapbox → monitor usage spikes Algolia → evaluate self-hosted at 100K

Where your platform lives

Every app runs on infrastructure somewhere. There are two fundamentally different approaches — and choosing wrong early is expensive to undo.

Traditional cloud

The warehouse model

Your app runs in one or a few large data centres. Powerful and flexible — but you need experts to configure and manage it. More control, more complexity.

AWS Google Cloud Azure
Edge / modern stack

The distributed model

Your app runs across dozens of locations worldwide, close to each user. Faster to launch, simpler to maintain — with tradeoffs at very large scale.

Vercel Supabase Netlify Cloudflare
Think of it like film distribution. Traditional cloud is owning a cinema — you control everything, but you build and manage it all. Edge hosting is streaming on a global platform — it just works, reaches everyone instantly, and the infrastructure is invisible to you. For a trip platform at launch, edge is the right call. As you pass 100K users, the strategy shifts and a hybrid approach starts making sense. Knowing when to make that call — that's the job.
